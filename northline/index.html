03

How priority works

The question behind every snow contract is "am I first or fortieth." Here's the actual order of operations, stated plainly.

RUN 01

Seasonal contracts

Signed routes are plowed first, in a fixed sequence set before the season. Your slot doesn't move when it snows.

RUN 02

Per-push accounts

Called in storm by storm. Served after the contract route clears, in the order requests land.

RUN 03

New / one-off calls

Fit in where the route allows. Honest answer: in a heavy storm, this is where the wait lives.

05

Method, not mascots

What gets a plow, what gets a shovel crew, and when the salt goes down. The parts that decide whether the lot is actually safe.

Plow

Driveways and lots cleared to pavement, banked away from doors and egress, not just pushed to the nearest edge.

Brine pre-treat

Applied ahead of the storm so snow doesn't bond to the surface. Less ice, less salt, faster clears.

Salt & sand

Metered by temperature and surface, logged by time and location. Enough to keep footing, not enough to kill the lawn.

Shovel crew

Walks, steps, and hydrant clearances a blade can't reach. Sent behind the plow, not instead of it.

07

The real questions

Pulled from what buyers actually ask before they sign, not a generic list.

Am I really first, or does everyone hear that? +
Contract routes run in a fixed sequence set before the season, and that sequence doesn't reshuffle when it snows. Per-push and one-off calls are served after the contract route clears. It's an order of operations, not a promise we repeat to everyone.
What depth triggers a dispatch? +
Contracts dispatch automatically at {{TRIGGER_INCHES}}" of accumulation, no call needed. You can set a lower trigger for a managed site with liability exposure. Below trigger we monitor and salt as conditions call for it.
Do you carry insurance and can my board see the certificate? +
Yes. A certificate of insurance is available on request for commercial and HOA accounts, along with timestamped service and salt-application logs your insurer or board can file.
What happens in an ice event with no snow? +
Ice is a separate dispatch from plowing. Brine pre-treat goes down ahead of a freezing event where forecast allows, and salt crews run on ice regardless of accumulation. Contract sites are covered for both.
Why is there no photo of your trucks or crew here? +
This is a design concept, so there's no real operation to photograph, and we won't stage stock models as staff. On a live build, fleet and job photos come from the operator under a scope-of-work warranty. Everything decorative here is drawn, not borrowed.
